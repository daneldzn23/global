# Apple

## Why it's a reference

Not for tone (Apple is consumer, Nelogica is B2B/institutional per
DESIGN.md) but for **craft mechanics**: how a real product photograph
or device mockup is lit, staged, and animated so it reads as premium
rather than templated. This is the antidote to V1.4's generic
"glassy card" and "stock photo" execution — the fix isn't to remove
imagery, it's to render it with real production value.

## What to steal

- **Device mockups treated as hero characters**: precise perspective,
  real reflections/shadows grounded in a light source, subtle parallax
  on scroll — directly supports DESIGN.md's "motion has a job" and
  "mockups behaving like real hardware."
- **One product, one full-bleed moment per section** — Apple rarely
  crams multiple ideas into one screen; each section earns its own
  scroll-stop. Useful discipline against overcrowding Nelogica's
  multi-platform (BlackArrow/Bookmap/Profit/OMS) story into busy grids.
- **Typography scale as the primary hierarchy tool**: huge, confident
  headline sizes with generous negative space, rather than a small
  eyebrow tag + medium headline + card grid repeated six times (the
  exact repetition DESIGN.md flags in V1.4).
- **Cross-device consistency shown literally**: the same product
  rendered across desktop/tablet/phone in one composition — maps
  directly to Nelogica's real "Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, Web"
  platform breadth claim.

## What NOT to copy

- Apple's minimalism trends toward *sparse* information density.
  DESIGN.md is explicit that Nelogica should stay dense/technical
  ("candlestick charts, order books... a feature of the design, not
  something to sand down for simplicity") — borrow Apple's staging and
  lighting technique, not its content minimalism.
- No stock lifestyle photography — Apple's own product shots are real
  hardware, which is the point; Nelogica's equivalent is real platform
  screenshots, not people-at-desks photography.

## Where to look

- Recent Mac/iPhone product pages — specifically the hero device
  render and the "designed for" cross-device section.
